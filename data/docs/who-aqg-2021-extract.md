## Purpose and legal status

WHO describes the 2021 Global Air Quality Guidelines as health-based recommendations and interim targets for key pollutants. Governments may use them as targets while considering technical capacity, economic capacity, air-quality policy and local political and social conditions.

The guidelines are not Indonesian legislation and do not change the legal status of a national or DKI standard. An answer should label a comparison as a health-guideline comparison, not as a finding that a Jakarta operator violated Indonesian law.

## Recommended particulate levels

The WHO table reports a PM2.5 annual guideline level of 5 micrograms per cubic metre and a 24-hour guideline level of 15 micrograms per cubic metre. The 24-hour recommendation is defined at the 99th percentile, equivalent to approximately three to four exceedance days per year.

For PM10, the table reports an annual guideline level of 15 micrograms per cubic metre and a 24-hour guideline level of 45 micrograms per cubic metre. These values use an averaging time and pollutant-specific unit that must remain attached to the comparison.

## Interim targets and uncertainty

WHO provides interim targets as progressive steps for places where pollution is high. The guideline does not state that concentrations below a threshold have zero health effects; it is a population-health recommendation and should not be used to diagnose an individual.

The guideline page says the 2021 update reflects stronger evidence of health effects at lower concentrations than earlier guidance. Source apportionment, exposure and intervention decisions still require local data and methods; the guideline alone does not identify Jakarta's emission sources.
