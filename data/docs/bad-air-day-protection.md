## What to do now

1. Check a fresh reading and timestamp on the official DKI portal (and the
   pollutant, not only the index). If the reading is elevated, shorten outdoor
   time, move exercise to a cleaner time/place, and avoid moderate-to-vigorous
   outdoor exertion. Jakarta's own seasonal notice says low rain and wind in
   the dry season can allow PM2.5 to accumulate; this is local context, not a
   forecast for every day.
2. Make the cleanest indoor room the place where children, older adults, and
   people with heart or lung disease spend more time. Keep windows and doors
   closed while outdoor particles are higher, but ventilate when outdoor air is
   cleaner. Closing windows is not a complete solution: cooking, tobacco,
   incense, candles, and other indoor sources can still raise exposure.
3. If a short, unavoidable high-particle exposure requires a respirator, use a
   certified, approved particulate respirator (for example an approved N95 or
   equivalent) that seals over the nose and mouth. Put it on correctly, keep it
   on throughout exposure, replace it when saturated or damaged, and do not
   treat a loose surgical/cloth mask as equivalent. A particulate respirator
   does not remove gases, and a poor seal defeats the rating.
4. For indoor particles, a portable cleaner can help when it is sized for the
   room, operated at a useful fan speed, and maintained. It supplements source
   control and cleaner-air ventilation; it cannot make a room pollutant-free.

## Limits and escalation

WHO's review found portable filters can lower indoor PM2.5 in real-world
studies, but health evidence is limited and devices have cost, noise,
maintenance, infiltration, and ozone/gas-removal limitations. It found little
evidence of health benefit from respirators in the general public and does not
recommend routine respirator use as a population measure; short controlled
high-exposure situations are a different risk-management question. People with
cardiorespiratory disease should ask a clinician before using a respirator or
changing activity. This assistant cannot diagnose symptoms. Seek urgent care
for severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, fainting, blue lips, or rapidly
worsening symptoms; persistent or concerning symptoms warrant a clinician.

Personal protection reduces a person's dose. It does not reduce Jakarta's
emissions and cannot replace enforcement or clean-air policy. [bad-air-day-protection]
