# Evidence-based policy improvements for Jakarta
Publisher: Dinas Lingkungan Hidup DKI Jakarta; World Health Organization
Source: https://www.jakarta.go.id/siaran-pers/4802-SP-HMS-06-2024
Published: 2024-06-19; WHO personal-actions brief 2024-11-15
Status as of: 2026-09-07 (priority options; implementation and outcomes require evaluation)
Jurisdiction: Jakarta and the Greater Jakarta airshed
Document type: policy_options
Tags: topic=policy-improvement; audience=decision-maker-and-public; action=accountability

Priorities should be separated by mechanism and evaluated with dated baseline,
coverage, compliance, emissions, and ambient-health indicators:

* Near term enforcement: publish a vehicle-testing denominator and pass/fail
  and sanction rates; expand roadside checks and repair support; audit testing
  centres; require transparent stationary-source inspection and CEMS follow-up.
  DKI's own 2024 materials identify inventory and oversight as work in
  progress, so report completion rather than announce activity as impact.
* Transport: make public transport reliable and affordable, target the
  dirtiest high-use vehicles, control idling and freight routes, and evaluate
  low-emission zones or road pricing with an enacted legal basis, exemptions,
  equity protections, and measured emissions—not only traffic speed.
* Industry and power: enforce permits and stack standards, publish CEMS and
  inspection data, investigate coal and industrial contributions, and use
  corrective orders and sanctions with follow-through. Attribution must retain
  uncertainty across source-apportionment studies.
* Regional coordination: use one airshed inventory and synchronized plans with
  Bogor, Depok, Tangerang, Bekasi, and Cianjur; include meteorology and
  upwind industrial sources. A Jakarta-only intervention cannot control every
  transported plume.
* Monitoring and transparency: add calibrated stations and open timestamped
  raw data, QA/QC, station siting, missingness, emissions inventories, and
  programme evaluations. Keep ISPU, PM2.5 concentration, and health indicators
  distinct.
* Health protection: publish clear episode thresholds and advice, prioritize
  schools, outdoor workers, older adults, children, and people with heart/lung
  disease, and make clean indoor air available. WHO stresses that these
  personal measures are a supplement; source reduction remains the primary,
  equitable intervention.

The evidence supports these as defensible options and measurement requirements,
not a guaranteed causal ranking. [policy-improvements-evidence]
