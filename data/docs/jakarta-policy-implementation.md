## Rule on paper

Pergub DKI No. 66/2020 is listed as berlaku by both BPK and DKI JDIH and
became effective 2020-12-24. It requires emission testing arrangements for
motor vehicles and sets inspection/control responsibilities. PP No. 22/2021
and Permen LHK No. 14/2020 are national instruments for ambient environmental
management and ISPU methodology. These instruments have different legal
force; a strategy, governor instruction, and health guideline are not the same
as a binding regulation.

## What implementation evidence actually says

In its 2024 public statement, DKI said implementation of Kepgub 576/2023's
Strategy for Air Pollution Control was still being completed: the city was
developing a more systematic emissions inventory, tightening mobile and
stationary-source oversight, and seeking cooperation with Bogor, Depok,
Tangerang, Bekasi, and Cianjur. DKI's official CEMS page publishes stationary-
source monitoring/evaluation reports for 2020–2024. These are evidence of
program activity and identified needs, not proof that all sources comply or
that pollution fell because of one measure.

The DKI emissions-testing FAQ says testing is mandatory for passenger cars and
motorcycles older than three years under Pergub 66/2020 and that failure can
lead to a traffic ticket under national traffic law. [jakarta-emission-testing]
A DKI/Vital Strategies
2024 survey report discusses barriers and support for enforcement; it is
attitudinal evidence, not a compliance rate. The public pages reviewed here do
not provide a complete, independently audited denominator for all vehicles,
all industrial stacks, inspection frequency, or sanction follow-through. Those
are material evidence gaps. [jakarta-cems-monitoring]

## Courts, coordination, and cautious inference

The official Supreme Court case directory records citizen lawsuit
374/Pdt.G/LH/2019/PN Jkt.Pst (decision 2021-09-16). [jakarta-court-air-quality]
It is evidence of a court
finding about public duties and regional coordination; it is not evidence that
every ordered action was completed. DKI itself describes regional transport,
industry, meteorology, and airshed contributions, so a Jakarta-only rule may
have limited effect when emissions originate outside the province. This is a
mechanism and governance constraint, not proof that any single agency caused
a particular reading.

Therefore, “regulations are not working” should be answered as a testable gap:
rules exist, but coverage, inspections, reliable data, sanctions, budgets,
inter-agency authority, and regional implementation must be measured before
claiming failure. [jakarta-policy-implementation]
